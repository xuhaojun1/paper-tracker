Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have become an important paradigm for robotic manipulation by unifying visual perception, language understanding, and action generation. Since reliable manipulation requires sufficient observation of objects, end-effectors, and their spatial relationships, multi-view inputs are widely used to provide complementary spatial information. However, our experiments show that existing VLA models do not fully exploit such multi-view complementarity. The policy tends to persistently rely on the wrist view, lacking the ability to dynamically adjust global-local view dependency across manipulation stages, which limits stage adaptability and robustness. To address this issue, we propose FocusVLA, a stage-aware multi-view routing framework. FocusVLA predicts future interaction intent from historical actions and uses it to guide dynamic view routing during action generation. By adaptively coordinating global and local observations across different task stages, FocusVLA improves multi-view utilization. On RoboTwin 2.0, FocusVLA improves the average success rate over the strongest baseline π_{0.5} by 10.10 pp. In real-world evaluation, FocusVLA improves the success rate from 71.7\% to 85.0\%, achieving a 13.3\% gain over π_{0.5}. Moreover, the intent prediction and dynamic routing modules of FocusVLA can be integrated into existing VLA frameworks without modifying their core architectures.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To learn these signals without fine-grained progress annotations, Echo constructs successful, failed, and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision properties. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

Abstract
Building virtual embodied agents that operate in open-ended games like human players is a long-standing goal requiring both long-horizon autonomy and reliable native-interface control. As a procedurally generated open world, Minecraft provides a natural testbed for studying the integration of these capabilities. Existing agents typically provide either native visuomotor control without long-horizon autonomy or high-level planning executed through environment APIs or predefined skills. To bridge this gap, we propose HiSTEVE, a hierarchical VLA agent with a capability-aware agent harness. At the orchestration level, we equip the harness with structured memory for tracking progress and a VLM planner for selecting subtasks within the VLA's capabilities. To reliably determine subtask completion, we further introduce a policy-coupled task completion state detector that conditions on VLA policy context to signal candidate completion. At the control level, a single VLA executes all subtasks with vision-only perception and native keyboard-and-mouse actions. To mitigate interference between heterogeneous GUI and non-GUI interactions, we introduce regime-specific RMSNorm branches and separate mouse-movement token embeddings. We also train the VLA through world knowledge injection, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with environment feedback to improve reliability and recovery. HiSTEVE achieves state-of-the-art atomic-task performance, scoring 85.0% on MineStudio Bench and a 37.4% average success rate on OpenHA, while also completing long-horizon tasks such as crafting an iron pickaxe from scratch like human players.

Abstract
Existing vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable for fine-grained visual perception. In this report, we present WeVisOne-VL (WeChat Vision All-in-One), a powerful VLM that unifies object-level and pixel-level perception through structured next-token prediction. It supports multiple visual tasks including object detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting, enabling localization at any user-specific granularity, from points and boxes to masks and alpha mattes. Architecturally, we integrate a lightweight pixel decoder into the VLM via a special <OBJ> token to generate instance-level pixel outputs. Across over 30 benchmarks, WeVisOne-VL demonstrates leading performance on both object-level and pixel-level tasks, while maintaining robust rejection for object-absent queries. By releasing WeVisOne-VL to the open-source community, we hope to advance VLMs from broad visual understanding toward truly reliable perception systems for real-world deployment.

Abstract
Video foundation models have advanced rapidly in understanding and generating visual sequences, while video world models seek to capture the underlying dynamics of the world they depict. Despite their different objectives, both concern the relationship between video observations and an evolving world. This survey adopts world modeling as a unifying lens for examining these developments. Viewing an observed video as a partial, lossy, and temporally ordered projection of a latent temporal world, we organize the literature around four complementary operations: reading infers world properties from observed video; writing synthesizes video from specified or learned world conditions; sharing integrates understanding and generation through common modeling mechanisms; and interacting predicts and updates world states under actions and evolving observations. This perspective distinguishes temporal coherence in observed or generated sequences from consistency in the underlying world state. Across tasks, representations, models, datasets, and benchmarks, we trace a shift in emphasis from clip-level recognition and perceptual fidelity toward persistent state, controllable evolution, physical and causal validity, and evaluation grounded in interaction and downstream decisions, and we distill the open challenges that follow.

First: 2026-10-22T04:43:48.000Z · Latest: 2026-10-23T00:00:00.000Z
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Cinematic video generation requires both temporal inference from limited inputs (i.e., sparse rather than frame-by-frame guidance) and factor-aware control of character, scene, action, and camera. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer useful priors for both capabilities, but their representations are not automatically actionable for video generation: a Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) may bypass them through native conditioning pathways, and using them does not ensure either factor-aware control or generation-compatible temporal prediction. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL), which establishes effective MLLM latent use as a shared prerequisite and then targets the two capabilities with complementary learning signals. First, latent-only generator grounding trains the DiT with a frozen MLLM and connector, strengthening the DiT's use of MLLM latents for generation. Second, for factor-aware control, paired videos specify a target-factor change and non-target preservation, and factor-span replacement trains the DiT to realize this relationship in its output. Third, for temporal inference, text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations share a video target, and the trained DiT is then frozen while the MLLM and connector are optimized through it. This final stage trains limited-input representations for generation while retaining complete-observation reconstruction as a complementary training mode. Extensive experiments demonstrate our consistently better performance on three cinematic video generation tasks (i.e., sparse-to-video, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation) over baselines and validate FAPL's contributions to MLLM feature use, temporal control, and factor coordination.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
Recursive self-improvement (RSI) describes a system that improves not only its behavior but also the process by which it improves. Recent digital agents test this idea by rewriting their own code and checking each version with executable tests. Bringing these mechanisms into the physical world requires learning from partial observations through costly trials, while accounting for changing operating conditions and potentially irreversible consequences. We position RSI within robot learning and trace pathways from improving robot capabilities to improving the processes that generate them. We examine how physical experience can strengthen these improvement mechanisms and when the resulting gains can support further self-improvement of the mechanisms themselves. Gains carry forward only when feedback addresses remaining learning needs, the receiving component can use the change, its value survives changes in tasks or hardware, and continued practice remains safe and affordable. Existing studies, concentrated in manipulation, support behavioral improvement, benefits to later learning, and procedure improvement. Some systems also change the improver's knowledge or tools, but the physical studies reviewed here have yet to isolate gains attributable to recursion. We synthesize these findings into evaluation principles and research directions for machines that, trial by trial, safely improve the way they improve.

Abstract
Agent-evolution methods use execution feedback to improve prompts, skills, tools, and orchestration, but their implementations often rebuild the same experimental machinery. We present RSIHub, an open-source framework for building, comparing, and extending these methods on shared infrastructure. Recipes compose reusable operators and declare mutable resources; RSIHub supplies execution, evaluation, recovery, and lineage. Researchers can focus on evolution policy while evaluation stays outside the mutable surface and results remain tied to candidates and evaluation conditions. We instantiate AHE, Hyperagents, A-Evolve, and GEPA without modifying the framework core, keeping method-specific logic in recipes and operators. We evaluate them with MiniSWE and Codex on Terminal Bench 2 and τ^3 Banking under fixed model and evaluator configurations. The largest observed overall pass@1 gain is 13.5 percentage points, while method rankings vary across settings and optimization gains do not always transfer to held-out tasks. RSIHub provides a common protocol for comparing evolution policies, inspectable candidate histories, and reusable components for developing new methods.

Abstract
LLM memory is widely used in long-term conversation understanding. Agentic memory systems often use mutation, structural organization, learned retrieval strategies, or other complex operations. Is this complexity always necessary? We study Raw-RAG, a naive RAG baseline using dense retrieval and reranking over raw history to supply evidence to a reader LLM. Across LoCoMo, LongMemEval, MEME, and MemoryAgentBench, Raw-RAG ranks first among the compared memory systems in six of seven benchmark-model settings, outperforming the strongest compared agentic memory system by 0.13-5.53 points. We investigate why raw-history retrieval remains competitive. Evidence analysis shows that its higher coverage of necessary evidence can offset lower utilization. Useful evidence includes both answer values and the relations and dependency paths needed to derive them. Even complete access to transformed memory stores leaves an accuracy gap relative to raw evidence, highlighting the importance of preserving raw information. These findings challenge the necessity of complex agentic memory for the evaluated conversation tasks and establish naive RAG as an essential baseline.

Abstract
This paper studies category-level RGB-D object pose estimation, which recovers an object's 3D rotation and translation without instance-specific CAD models or reference views. This task is highly ambiguous due to intra-class variations, symmetries, and partial observations. Current solutions struggle to balance reliability and efficiency: methods based on intermediate structures are prone to error propagation, while diffusion models suffer from high inference latency and complex post-hoc candidate extraction. To address this, we present F2G-Pose, a real-time direct, single-pass framework that predicts category-level pose, metric size, and camera-frame completed shape from a single RGB-D observation in one feed-forward pass. F2G-Pose lifts dense visual foundation features to partial point clouds, encodes local geometry with DGCNN tokenization, and integrates object-level structure with a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer. Joint size prediction and camera-frame shape completion encourage metric consistency through dense geometric supervision. With its task-specific network trained only on pose-annotated synthetic SOPE data and its frozen RADIO backbone externally pretrained, F2G-Pose achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SOPE and ROPE while running at 28 FPS for pose-only inference, and further demonstrates strong camera-frame geometry on HouseCat6D and real-object transfer on HANDAL. Analyses of latency, robustness, shape completion, and GenPose++ candidate behavior further demonstrate the efficiency and reliability of F2G-Pose, with real-world manipulation experiments demonstrating deployment potential under segmented RGB-D inputs. The project page, code, and pretrained models are made available at https://jrryzh.github.io/F2G-Pose/.

Abstract
World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

Abstract

Abstract
We present IQuest-Q1, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) agentic foundation model with 320B total parameters (15B activated). Abstract
We introduce TWIG (Tree-structured Weights with Inherited Gates), a representation in which every weight of a neural network is the end of a path of small discrete decisions: a sign, a shift by a power of two, a zero gate, an index into a shared table. Weights that belong together share the upper part of their paths, and each decision is inherited by every weight below it, so it is stored once and applied once. This simple change has three consequences. Any trained network can be written as a TWIG, and when the decisions are powers of two its weight layers become circuits made almost entirely of wires and adders, suited to chips whose weights are fixed at fabrication. Pruning, quantization, low-rank factorization, codebooks and weight sharing become edits of one object, so a single optimizer can choose among all of them for a whole network. And choosing the decisions is the ground-state problem of a higher-order Boltzmann machine whose interactions follow the paths of the tree, the problem class that annealers and quantum optimizers are built to solve. Today these questions are handled apart: each compression method brings its own format and solver, a circuit with fixed weights needs a dedicated constant multiplier for every weight, and discrete solvers reach only narrow subproblems such as rounding. Weight formats have long been moving toward shared decisions, through group scales, shared exponents, microexponents and sparse weights; TWIG takes this trend to its end, and every common format is a shallow TWIG at no extra cost. On five weight matrices of a large language model, a flat tree whose signed power-of-two terms are chosen per weight for circuit cost, with no group scales, needs 9--15\% fewer additions than GPTQ at equal layer error at 3- and 4-bit accuracy on typical matrices, and 20--55\% fewer on matrices with strong outliers.
